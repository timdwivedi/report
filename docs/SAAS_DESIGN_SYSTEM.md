## Core Philosophy

### The Single Reason People Don't Convert

**They have unanswered objections.** Every unanswered question is a lost conversion. For every person who asks a question, there are 20+ who didn't bother asking and just left.

### The Bridge Concept

Your landing page builds a bridge across the chasm between where visitors are and where they want to go. Each answered objection is a plank in that bridge. Without it, only the most desperate prospects will make the leap.

### The Investment Mindset

Show the problem BEFORE showing the product. Like a great sales call, diagnose before you prescribe. When visitors feel the pain of their current situation, your solution becomes irresistible.

### Curiosity Over Explanation

The top of the page creates curiosity to keep scrolling. It does NOT explain the entire product. That's the most common mistake in SaaS landing pages.

> "If it's not clear in your head, it's not going to be clear on your page."

## The 9-Section Landing Page Formula

This is the proven structure. Every section has a purpose. Every section answers objections and builds the bridge.

### Section 1: HERO

**Goal:** Create curiosity and communicate the core outcome. NOT to explain the whole product.

**Structure:**
- Headline (result-focused, using formulas below)
- Subheadline (one sentence on HOW, creates curiosity)
- Primary CTA button (prominent, high contrast)
- Optional secondary CTA (lower commitment)
- Visual: product screenshot or hero illustration

**Key Rules:**
- Focus on the OUTCOME, not features
- Mention the biggest objection/problem they face with current solutions
- Make it specific, not vague
- One sentence max for how the product achieves this
- Clear, visible CTA above the fold

**What NOT to do:**
- Don't try to explain the entire product
- Don't use vague language like "We help businesses grow"
- Don't focus on features
- Don't use corporate buzzwords

---

### Section 2: SOCIAL PROOF BAR

**Goal:** Instant credibility before they read anything else.

**Options (pick one):**
- Logo bar of well-known companies in the target niche
- Big stat: "Over X customers" or "Y results delivered"
- "Trusted by X+ [target user type]"

**Rules:**
- Logos should be in grayscale, color on hover
- Numbers should be specific (not "lots of customers")
- Companies should be recognizable to the target audience

---

### Section 3: PROBLEM SECTION

**Goal:** Create an investment mindset BEFORE showing the product. Remind them of the pain. Make them realize their current situation is untenable.

**Structure:**

**Part A: Critique Their Current Way**
- Show why their current method (spreadsheets, manual processes, doing it themselves) sucks
- Use X bullets for each reason it's broken
- Quantify the cost: "How Much Is This Costing You?"
- Make them feel the weight of the problem

**Part B: Critique Competitor Solutions**
- Show why other tools on the market aren't ideal
- Address why they haven't already bought from competitors
- What features/benefits are missing from alternatives

**Title Formula:** "Honestly Now, Is This The Way To [Fix Problem/Get Result]?"

**Why This Works:** This section warms them up to your solution by making them realize their current situation is untenable. Like a sales call where you diagnose before prescribing. By the time they reach the solution section, they're hungry for it.

---

### Section 4: SOLUTION / HOW IT WORKS

**Goal:** Show how the product delivers on the promise made in the hero.

**Structure: 3-5 Steps (No more, no less)**

**Critical Rule:** Explain in ORDER -- the actual journey from new customer to successful customer.

**Step 1 (Usually):** Integration/Import
- Addresses: "How long does it take to switch?"
- Addresses: "Does it integrate with my current tools?"

**Steps 2-4:** The actual process of achieving the result
- Each step has a title and supporting visual (screenshot/GIF)
- Answer objections immediately after each step
- Explain why this way is better than alternatives

**Step 5 (Usually):** The outcome/result they get
- Show what success looks like
- Visual proof if possible

**Rules:**
- Only explain how to get the RESULT, not every feature
- Use product screenshots or mockups to support each step
- Address objections immediately after each step
- Don't overwhelm -- give them just enough to understand the process
- Make them feel: "If I do these 3-5 things, I WILL get the result"

**Supporting Pattern:**
Alternate layout: image left + text right, then text left + image right. This creates visual rhythm and keeps the eye moving.

---

### Section 5: BENEFITS / SWITCHING SECTION

**Goal:** Highlight what makes switching easy and reduce perceived risk.

**Include:**
- Fast setup/migration time
- Easy integrations with existing tools
- Quality of support
- Seamless onboarding
- Whatever unique benefits reduce the commitment to switch

**Key Principle:** Address the switching costs. People fear change. Remove that fear.

---

### Section 6: SOCIAL PROOF / TESTIMONIALS

**Goal:** Result-driven proof from people the target audience can relate to.

**What Makes a Good Testimonial:**
- Specific results: "We improved X by 40%"
- Specific metrics and outcomes
- From people who match the target persona
- Highlight the best parts of longer testimonials

**What to AVOID:**
- Generic praise: "Awesome tool!", "Our team loves it"
- Testimonials from people outside the target audience
- No metrics, no specifics

**If You Lack Testimonials:**
- Show number of users/trials
- Show usage statistics
- Show industry awards or recognitions
- Create realistic placeholder testimonials from the target industry (clearly mark as mock data)

**Title Formula:** "This Is Why X+ Customers Love [Product Name]"

---

### Section 7: FINAL CTA

**Goal:** Convert the visitors who are now convinced.

**Structure:**
- Urgency headline (FOMO): "These Companies Are Already Getting [RESULT], When Will You?"
- Risk reversal paragraph (2-3 sentences)
- Primary CTA button (same as hero)
- Trust elements as checkmarks below

**Risk Reversal Examples:**
- "Start your 14-day free trial"
- "No credit card required"
- "Cancel anytime, no questions asked"
- "We'll do the setup for you"
- "Monthly contracts, cancel anytime"
- "If you don't see results, you don't pay"

**Break Down Next Steps:**
- Book a FREE demo with our team
- We'll do the setup for you
- Monthly contracts, cancel at any time

---

### Section 8: ALTERNATIVE CTA

**Goal:** Convert people who aren't ready to buy NOW.

**Why This Matters:** Most visitors aren't ready to convert immediately. You either provide alternatives or lose them to competitors forever. These leads might be ready in a few months. Nurture them.

**Options:**
- Lead magnet download
- Newsletter signup
- Watch a demo video
- Read a case study
- Schedule a consultation call
- Browse feature pages

**Title Formula:** "Not Ready To Commit? No Problem."

---

### Section 9: FOOTER

**Goal:** Clean, professional close with essential links.

**Include:**
- App name + copyright year
- Navigation: Privacy Policy, Terms of Service, Contact
- Social media links (if applicable)
- High-contrast treatment (typically dark background with light text, but adapt to brand)
- Simple and clean

## Messaging Principles

### Use Customer Language, Not Corporate Speak

The biggest messaging mistake: using YOUR words instead of THEIRS.

| Corporate Speak | Customer Language |
|----------------|-------------------|
| "Reduce customer churn" | "Stop losing 10% of my revenue every month" |
| "Advanced AI-powered platform" | "Automate follow-ups without changing your process" |
| "Comprehensive solution for businesses" | "Manage all your leads in one place" |
| "Data-driven insights" | "Know exactly who's ready to buy" |
| "Leverage synergies" | Delete this. Start over. |

**The Test:** How do your actual customers describe the problem? Use THOSE exact words.

### The 3-Step Messaging Process

**Step 1: Understand Why People Buy**
Write down the top 3-5 reasons. If you can't do this in 5 minutes, the messaging won't be clear on the page either.
- What problems does it fix?
- What results do they get?
- What features do they love most?
- Why do they choose this over competitors?

**Step 2: Identify All Objections**
Listen to sales calls, read support tickets, check competitor reviews.
- How do people describe their problems?
- What keeps them from taking action?
- Have they looked at competitors? Why didn't they buy?
- What features do they want to see first?
- What misconceptions do they have?
- Why do they hesitate after seeming excited?

**Step 3: Answer Every Objection On The Page**
For every objection you miss, you lose conversions. Common objections every SaaS page must address:

1. **"What does this do?"** -> Clear headline about the outcome
2. **"Can it fix MY problem?"** -> Specific problem statements using their language
3. **"Does it work with my tools?"** -> Integration list in Step 1 of solution
4. **"Why is this better than what I have?"** -> Critique current solution section
5. **"Why is this better than competitors?"** -> Critique competitors section
6. **"Is it worth the time to switch?"** -> Quick setup, migration support
7. **"Is it worth the money?"** -> Cost of NOT fixing the problem
8. **"What if it doesn't work?"** -> Risk reversal (trial, guarantee, support)

### Positioning Principles

**Focus on Your Best Customers (80/20):**
- Who brings 80% of the revenue?
- Who's easiest to sell to?
- Who benefits from the product the most?
- Who's already paying for solutions to this problem?

**Niche-Specific Messaging Wins:**
- "The only CRM built for financial advisors" beats "A CRM for everyone"
- More specific = easier to convert = lower customer acquisition cost
- One client saw 40% increase in MRR in 30 days just by focusing on a niche

## Copywriting Formulas

### Hero Headlines

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Result + Without Problem | "Automate your follow-ups without changing your sales process" |
| Niche-Specific | "The only cold email tool built for agencies" |
| Quantified Benefit | "Manage hundreds of thousands of leads without hiring" |
| Problem Reversal | "No more losing leads to bad follow-up timing" |
| Outcome Statement | "Turn your pipeline into a machine that books demos while you sleep" |

### Subheadlines

**Purpose:** Provide context without overexplaining. One sentence that makes the headline more concrete.

**Pattern:** "[How it works in one sentence that addresses the main switching objection]"

Example:
- Headline: "Automate your follow-ups without changing your sales process"
- Subheadline: "Our AI adapts to your existing workflow in under 5 minutes"

### Problem Section Headlines

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Challenge | "Honestly, Is This The Way To Scale Your Outreach?" |
| Cost | "How Much Is Manual Follow-Up Costing You?" |
| Frustration | "Tired of Losing Leads Because Nobody Followed Up?" |

### Solution Section Headlines

| Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|
| Promise | "This Is How [Product] Fixes All Of That" |
| Steps | "Get From Zero To Booked Demos In 3 Steps" |
| Simple | "Here's How It Actually Works" |

### CTA Button Copy

**Instead of:** Submit, Learn More, Click Here, Get Started

**Use:**
- "Start Free Trial"
- "Book Free Demo"
- "Get Started Free"
- "See It In Action"
- "Try It Free for 14 Days"

**Make it specific to the outcome, not generic.**

### Body Copy Rules

- Short paragraphs (2-3 sentences max)
- Use bullets when listing benefits
- Bold key phrases sparingly
- Focus on benefits, not features
- Use "you" language (talk TO them, not about them)

## Design Specifications

### Layout

| Property | Value | Notes |
|----------|-------|-------|
| Max content width | 1200px | Centered container |
| Text max width | 800px | For readability in prose sections |
| Section padding (desktop) | 80px vertical | `py-20` in Tailwind |
| Section padding (mobile) | 48px vertical | `py-12` in Tailwind |
| Container horizontal padding | 24px | `px-6` in Tailwind |
| Mobile breakpoint | 768px | `md:` in Tailwind |
| Grid gap (cards/columns) | 32px | `gap-8` in Tailwind |

### Typography

| Element | Desktop | Mobile | Weight | Line Height |
|---------|---------|--------|--------|-------------|
| H1 (Hero headline) | 56px / `text-5xl` | 36px / `text-4xl` | 700 / `font-bold` | `leading-tight` |
| H2 (Section headline) | 40px / `text-4xl` | 28px / `text-3xl` | 700 / `font-bold` | `leading-tight` |
| H3 (Step titles) | 28px / `text-2xl` | 22px / `text-xl` | 600 / `font-semibold` | `leading-snug` |
| Body text | 18px / `text-lg` | 18px / `text-lg` | 400 / `font-normal` | 1.6 / `leading-relaxed` |
| Small text | 16px / `text-base` | 16px / `text-base` | 400 | `leading-normal` |

**Font Selection:** Choose fonts that match the brand's vertical and tone. Use the `ui-ux-pro-max` search tool (`--domain typography`) to find industry-appropriate pairings. Avoid defaulting to overused AI-generation fonts (Inter, Roboto, Space Grotesk) -- every project should feel distinct. Pair a characterful display font for headings with a refined body font.

### Color System

Every Bloom app defines its own palette during setup. The system should use these color tokens:

| Token | Usage | Tailwind |
|-------|-------|----------|
| `primary-500` | Main brand color, section backgrounds | `bg-primary-500` |
| `primary-600/700` | Hover states, darker accents | `bg-primary-600` |
| `primary-50/100` | Light backgrounds, badges | `bg-primary-50` |
| `accent` | CTA buttons, attention-grabbing elements | `bg-accent-500` |
| `neutral-light` | Alternating section backgrounds | Use your theme's light neutral tone |
| `text-body` | Body text | Use your theme's body text color |
| `text-heading` | Headlines | Use your theme's heading color |
| `success` | Success checkmarks, positive indicators | Use your theme's success/positive color |
| `danger` | Error states, X marks in problem section | Use your theme's danger/negative color |

**Section Background Alternation (suggested default -- adapt to brand direction):**
```
Hero:         Light base or primary gradient (match brand energy)
Social Proof: Subtle contrast from hero (slightly darker or lighter)
Problem:      Base background
Solution:     Gentle gradient transition
Benefits:     Base background
Testimonials: Subtle contrast background
Final CTA:    Bold/primary treatment (high contrast, inverted text)
Alt CTA:      Base background
Footer:       Dark treatment (darkest tone in palette)
```
Dark-themed brands, bold verticals, or unique aesthetics should adapt this pattern entirely. The goal is visual rhythm between sections, not specific colors.

### Spacing Scale

```
4px   (1)   - Icon padding, tight gaps
8px   (2)   - Between related elements
16px  (4)   - Between headline and body
24px  (6)   - Container horizontal padding
32px  (8)   - Grid gaps, card padding
48px  (12)  - Section padding (mobile)
64px  (16)  - Between major content blocks

```

### Responsive Breakpoints

| Breakpoint | Width | Usage |
|-----------|-------|-------|
| Default | < 640px | Mobile-first base styles |
| `sm:` | 640px+ | Small tablets |

**Mobile-First Rule:** Every component MUST look great at 375px width. Build mobile-first, then add breakpoints for larger screens.

## Premium Visual Depth (4 Layers)

Premium dark UIs (Linear, Vercel, Stripe, Lovable) stack 4 visual layers to avoid the "flat dark page with text" look. These utilities are pre-built in `globals.css`.

### The 4 Layers

```
Layer 1: Dot/grid pattern with radial mask (spatial context)
Layer 2: Ambient gradient blobs (color depth, 0.08-0.15 opacity)
Layer 3: Noise/grain texture (organic feel, 3-5% opacity)
Layer 4: Content with depth (card shadows, gradient text, glows)
```

### Layer 1: Dot Grid

Add `<div className="dot-grid" />` inside any section with `position: relative`. Creates a subtle dot pattern that fades via radial mask — adds spatial context to empty areas like hero sections.

```jsx
<section className="relative py-24 overflow-hidden">
    <div className="dot-grid" />
    {/* Content here */}
</section>
```

### Layer 2: Ambient Glow Blobs

Large blurred divs using the brand's primary/accent colors at 0.08-0.15 opacity. These create color depth and visual interest behind content. Each section should have 1-2 blobs at different positions.

```jsx
{/* Inside a section with relative + overflow-hidden */}
<div className="absolute top-1/4 left-1/4 w-[600px] h-[600px] bg-primary/[0.08] rounded-full blur-[150px] pointer-events-none" />
<div className="absolute bottom-1/4 right-1/4 w-[400px] h-[400px] bg-accent/[0.05] rounded-full blur-[120px] pointer-events-none" />
```

**Critical: 0.08-0.15 opacity, NOT 0.02-0.04.** Low-opacity blobs are invisible. If you can't see them on the page, they're too faint.

### Layer 3: Noise Texture

Add `noise-overlay` class to the page wrapper. Adds a subtle SVG fractal noise at 3.5% opacity that breaks up flat color banding. Content inside must have `relative z-10` to sit above the noise pseudo-element.

```jsx
<div className="min-h-screen bg-background noise-overlay">
    <div className="relative z-10">
        {/* All page content here */}
    </div>
</div>
```

### Layer 4: Content Depth

#### Card Depth
Add `card-depth` class to any card element. Provides inner top highlight (simulates light source) + outer depth shadow.

```jsx
<div className="bg-card border border-border rounded-2xl p-7 backdrop-blur-xl card-depth">
```

#### Gradient Headlines
Add `text-gradient-headline` class to section h2 headlines. Creates a white-to-zinc vertical gradient. Includes `padding-bottom: 0.1em` to prevent descender clipping (g, y, p, q letters).

```jsx
<h2 className="text-3xl sm:text-4xl md:text-5xl font-bold text-gradient-headline">
    Your Headline Here
</h2>
```

For colored accent spans within a gradient headline, use Tailwind's built-in gradient text:
```jsx
<h2 className="text-gradient-headline">
    Build Faster.{" "}
    <span className="text-transparent bg-clip-text bg-gradient-to-r from-primary to-accent">
        Ship Sooner.
    </span>
</h2>
```

#### Text Glow
Add `text-glow-primary` or `text-glow-accent` to headlines for a subtle color halo behind text. Pairs with gradient headlines.

#### Section Dividers
Add decorative `h-px` gradient lines between sections for visual rhythm:
```jsx
<div className="h-px bg-gradient-to-r from-transparent via-primary/20 to-transparent" />
```

### When to Use Each Layer

| Context | Layers to Use |
|---------|---------------|
| Landing page / marketing | All 4 layers |
| Dashboard / app UI | Layer 4 only (card-depth, subtle shadows) |
| Hero sections | Layers 1 + 2 + 3 (dot grid + blobs + noise) |
| Card grids | Layer 4 (card-depth + hover effects) |
| Dark-mode pages | All 4 layers (noise is most impactful on dark) |
| Light-mode pages | Layers 2 + 4 (skip noise + dot grid) |

### Performance Notes

- Noise overlay is a static SVG — no JS, no animation, ~200 bytes
- Dot grid uses pure CSS `radial-gradient` — zero performance cost
- Glow blobs use `blur-[120px+]` — browser composites these efficiently when `pointer-events: none` is set
- `backdrop-blur-xl` on cards IS expensive — use `backdrop-blur-sm` on lists with 5+ items (e.g. FAQ accordions)
- `transition-all` is an anti-pattern — specify exact properties: `transition-[border-color,background-color,box-shadow]`

### Common Mistakes

1. **Glow blobs too faint** — 0.02-0.04 opacity is invisible. Use 0.08-0.15.
2. **Missing `relative z-10`** — Content inside `noise-overlay` must sit above the `::after` pseudo-element (z-index: 1).
3. **`transition-all` on lists** — Transitions every CSS property. Specify only what changes.
4. **`backdrop-blur-xl` on every card** — Each is a separate compositing layer. Use sparingly or reduce to `backdrop-blur-sm`.
5. **Gradient text clipping descenders** — Always use `.text-gradient-headline` (has `padding-bottom: 0.1em` built in), not raw `background-clip: text`.
6. **Badge "cutout" backgrounds** — When a badge straddles a card border (e.g. "RECOMMENDED" pill at `-top-3`), the background behind the badge must exactly match the page background or the card border shows through as a strikethrough. Use `bg-[var(--background)]` and add `px-2 py-0.5` padding.

## Component Library

### Sticky Header

```
- Height: 80px (h-20)
- Background: White with shadow on scroll
- Logo: Left aligned
- CTA Button: Right aligned, accent color
- Position: sticky top-0 z-50
- Transition: Add shadow-md when scrolled past 0
```

### Hero Section

```
- Layout: 2-column on desktop (text-heavy side + visual), stack on mobile
- Text alignment: Left on desktop, center on mobile (adapt to brand)
- Image: Product screenshot or illustration with depth (shadow, border, rounding)
- Vertical padding: Generous (80-120px desktop, 48-80px mobile)
- CTA buttons: Primary + optional secondary, visually grouped
```

### Problem Section Bullets

```
- Use X icon (danger color from theme) for current way / competitor critiques
- Each bullet: flex items-start with consistent gap
- Icon: theme danger color
- Text: theme body text color
- Spacing: consistent vertical rhythm between bullets

- Cost callout box: light danger background, left border accent, padded, rounded
```

### Solution Steps

```
- Alternate layout: image + text, flip direction for each step (creates rhythm)
- Step badge: light primary background, primary text, pill shape
- Step title: prominent heading weight
- Step copy: body text in muted/secondary tone
- Step image: product screenshot with depth (shadow, border, rounding)
- Optional glow/highlight effect behind image using primary color at low opacity
- Spacing: generous separation between steps
```

### Benefit Cards

```
- Grid: responsive columns (1 mobile, 2 tablet, 3-4 desktop)
- Each card: text-center
- Icon: circular background using success/primary color at low opacity, icon in full color
- Title: semibold, slightly larger than body
- Description: muted body text
```

### Testimonial Cards

```
- Grid: responsive columns (1 mobile, 2-3 desktop)
- Card: card background, rounded, padded, subtle shadow, subtle border
- Avatar: rounded-full (image or initials circle)
- Name: semibold
- Title/Company: small muted text
- Quote: body text color, italic
- Highlight key metric in bold
- Optional: star rating above quote
```

### CTA Buttons

```
Primary CTA:
- Background: accent/CTA color from theme
- Text: high contrast against background
- Padding: generous horizontal + vertical
- Border radius: match brand rounding (sharp for brutalist, rounded for friendly)
- Font: semibold, slightly larger than body
- Shadow: colored shadow using accent at low opacity
- Hover: subtle lift or shade shift
- Transition: smooth (200-300ms)

Secondary CTA:
- Border: accent color outline
- Text: accent color
- Background: transparent
- Same sizing as primary
- Hover: subtle fill with accent at low opacity
```

### Trust Elements (below CTA)

```
- Flex centered with consistent gap
- Each: icon + small muted text
- Checkmark icon in success/positive color
- Examples: "14-day free trial", "No credit card required", "Cancel anytime"
```

### Footer

```
- Background: darkest tone from theme palette
- Text: muted/secondary text color
- Layout: centered, simple
- Links: Privacy | Terms | Contact
- Bottom: Copyright + year
- Padding: generous vertical
```

## Common Mistakes

### 1. Feature Dumping
**Problem:** Listing every feature instead of focusing on outcomes.
**Fix:** Focus on 3-5 key benefits that drive the result. Everything else can be discovered after signup.

### 2. Vague Language
**Problem:** "We help businesses grow" -- What does this even mean?
**Fix:** "We help SaaS companies get 5+ qualified demos per day."

### 3. Explaining Too Much Too Soon
**Problem:** Trying to explain the entire product in the hero.
**Fix:** Create curiosity with the outcome, explain later in the Solution section.

### 4. Using YOUR Words Instead of THEIRS
**Problem:** "Reduce customer churn" (corporate speak)
**Fix:** "Stop losing 10% of my revenue every month" (their pain)

### 5. Not Addressing Objections
**Problem:** Visitors leave with unanswered questions.
**Fix:** Map out all objections and answer them proactively in the Problem and Solution sections.

### 6. Redesigning Without Fixing Root Problems
**Problem:** Just changing words but not addressing real objections.
**Fix:** Do the research first. Identify actual problems. Then write the page.

### 7. No Risk Reversal
**Problem:** Asking for commitment without reducing risk.
**Fix:** Free trial, money-back guarantee, no credit card required, "we'll do setup for you."

### 8. Making It Look Like a Template
**Problem:** Generic startup template with placeholder text.
**Fix:** Every element should feel specific to THIS product for THIS audience. Use industry-specific language, relevant testimonials, and real-looking data.

### 9. Ignoring Mobile
**Problem:** Looks great on desktop, broken on phone.
**Fix:** Build mobile-first. Test at 375px width. Stack all multi-column layouts on mobile.

### 10. Weak CTAs
**Problem:** "Submit", "Learn More", "Click Here"
**Fix:** "Start Free Trial", "Book Free Demo", "See It In Action"

## Conversion Checklist

Before launching, verify every item:

### Positioning
- [ ] Clear who this is for (and who it's NOT for)
- [ ] Focused on specific niche/ICP
- [ ] Unique value proposition is obvious within 5 seconds

### Hero Section
- [ ] Result-focused headline (not feature-focused)
- [ ] Addresses main objection
- [ ] Creates curiosity to keep scrolling
- [ ] CTA is clear, visible, and above the fold
- [ ] Social proof or big stat included

### Problem Section
- [ ] Core problem clearly stated in customer language
- [ ] Cost of not fixing is quantified
- [ ] Current solution/way is critiqued
- [ ] Competitor solutions are critiqued (without naming them directly)

### Solution Section
- [ ] 3-5 steps shown in chronological order
- [ ] Each step has supporting visual (screenshot/mockup)
- [ ] Objections answered after each step
- [ ] Focuses on getting the RESULT (not all features)

### Benefits Section
- [ ] Highlights what makes switching easy
- [ ] Addresses switching costs directly
- [ ] Reduces perceived risk

### Social Proof
- [ ] Result-driven testimonials with specific metrics
- [ ] From relatable people in the target audience
- [ ] Specific numbers and outcomes highlighted

### Final CTA
- [ ] Primary CTA repeated (same as hero)
- [ ] Risk reversal is strong and specific
- [ ] Next steps are crystal clear
- [ ] Trust elements (trial, no CC, cancel anytime)

### Alternative CTA
- [ ] Lower-commitment option for not-ready visitors
- [ ] Lead capture mechanism (email, demo video, case study)

### Overall
- [ ] Uses customer language throughout (not corporate speak)
- [ ] All major objections answered
- [ ] No vague or generic statements
- [ ] Focuses on outcomes, not features
- [ ] Mobile-responsive (tested at 375px width)
- [ ] Fast loading (no unnecessary animations or heavy images)

## For AI Agents

### When Building a Landing Page (Agent 2)

1. **Read this entire document first.** The 9-section structure is the proven default -- adapt section order, styling, and tone to the vertical and brand direction.
2. **Read the App Brief and Agent 2 Directives** in `docs/roadmap/01_project_spec.md` for colors, fonts, and tone.
3. **Follow the 9-section structure as your baseline.** Every section has a purpose. Adapt or merge sections when the vertical demands it.
4. **Use the copy formulas** for headlines -- don't improvise generic headlines.
5. **Make testimonials feel real.** Use job titles from the target industry. Include specific metrics.
6. **Follow the component specs** for spacing, typography, and layout.
7. **Mobile-first.** Every component must work at 375px width.
8. **No dynamic Tailwind classes.** Always use static class strings.
9. **Apply the 4 Premium Depth Layers** (see "Premium Visual Depth" section above):
   - Wrap the page in `noise-overlay`, content in `relative z-10`
   - Add `<div className="dot-grid" />` to hero section
   - Add 1-2 ambient glow blobs per section at **0.08-0.15 opacity** (NOT 0.02)
   - Use `card-depth` on all cards, `text-gradient-headline` on section h2s
   - Add `h-px` gradient dividers between sections
   - Use `transition-[specific-props]` instead of `transition-all`

### Headline Generation Process

When writing the hero headline:
1. Identify the #1 result the app delivers
2. Identify the #1 objection/problem users have with current solutions
3. Combine: "How to get [RESULT] without [PROBLEM]"
4. Refine to be specific and short (under 12 words ideal)

### Testimonial Generation Process

When creating mock testimonials:
1. Use realistic names from the target industry
2. Include a specific metric or result: "increased X by Y%"
3. Mention how quick/easy it was
4. Use job titles that match the target user persona
5. Include company name (can be fictional but industry-appropriate)
6. Highlight the key phrase in bold

### Problem Section Writing Process

1. List 3 reasons their current way sucks (manual, slow, expensive, error-prone)
2. Quantify the cost: "You're losing X hours/week" or "$X in missed revenue"
3. List 2-3 reasons competitors fall short (missing features, too expensive, wrong niche)
4. Transition: "There's a better way..."

### Solution Section Writing Process

1. Step 1: Setup/Integration (addresses "how long to switch?" objection)
2. Step 2-3: Core workflow (addresses "how does it actually work?" objection)
3. Step 4-5: Results/Outcome (addresses "will this work for me?" objection)
4. Each step: title + 2-3 sentences + visual description + objection addressed
